Is carmine (E120) halal?

Carmine, also called cochineal or E120, is a red dye made from insects. Scholars differ, so it is worth knowing what it is.

What is carmine?

Carmine is a red colour made from crushed cochineal insects. It is listed as E120, carmine, cochineal or natural red 4. It is found in some sweets, drinks, yoghurts, cosmetics and red coloured foods.

What do scholars say?

Scholars differ. Many hold that eating insects is not allowed, apart from locusts, so they treat carmine as haram. Others say that a dye used in a tiny amount is acceptable. Both views are held by respected scholars.

What does Halal Check UK do?

The app flags carmine so that you see it and can decide, and it tells you which ingredient caused the flag. If you follow the stricter view, avoid products with E120.

Alternatives

Look for plant colours such as beetroot red, paprika extract or anthocyanins, which are all plant based.

This page is a general guide, not a religious ruling. For a ruling, ask a qualified scholar.
