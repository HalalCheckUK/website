Three possible answers

Each one comes with the ingredients behind it.

Halal

Nothing in the ingredients list is a problem.

Doubtful

An ingredient could be fine or not, such as gelatine or E471, where the source isn't stated. Check the packaging for a halal mark.

Haram

Something that is not allowed, such as pork or alcohol, is in the list.

The app is a guide, not a religious ruling or a halal certificate. If you need a ruling, ask a qualified person. If you have an allergy, always read the label yourself.

Where the information comes from

Product names and ingredients come partly from Open Food Facts, a free, open database kept by volunteers, and partly from shoppers using the app.

When you photograph a label, an AI service reads the text. The photo is not kept. The ingredients are then checked against our own list of halal, doubtful and haram ingredients.

Something wrong?

Every product page has a report button, and you can tap "Is this result right?" on any result.

You can also email us at halalcheckuk@gmail.com.

Questions people ask

Short answers about how Halal Check UK works.

Is Halal Check UK free?

Yes. The app is free, has no ads and needs no account.

How does Halal Check UK decide if a food is halal?

It reads the ingredients list, from a barcode lookup or a photo of the label, and checks every ingredient against its own list of halal, doubtful and haram ingredients. The result shows which ingredient decided the answer.

Does a halal certificate change the result?

A halal certificate can clear ingredients whose source is unclear, such as gelatine or E471. Pork, blood and alcohol stay haram even with a certificate.

Is Halal Check UK a religious ruling?

No. It is a guide, not a fatwa or a halal certificate. For a ruling, ask a qualified scholar. If you have an allergy, always read the label yourself.

Is gelatine halal?

Gelatine is made from animal collagen, so whether it is halal depends on the animal and how it was prepared. Here is how to read the label.

Is E471 halal?

E471 appears in bread, cakes, ice cream and spreads. It can come from plants or animals, which is why the label alone does not tell you.

 Guide

Is carmine (E120) halal?

Carmine, also called cochineal or E120, is a red dye made from insects. Scholars differ, so it is worth knowing what it is.
