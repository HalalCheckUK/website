Three steps in the aisle

No sign-up and no waiting. Open the app and point the camera.

  1. Scan the barcode

    If the product is known you see the answer straight away, with the ingredients.

  2. Not found? Photograph the ingredients

    The app reads the list from the photo and checks every ingredient. You can save the product for the next shopper.

  3. Read the reason

    Flagged ingredients are marked, with a short plain explanation of why.

Three possible answers

Each one comes with the ingredients behind it.

Halal
Nothing in the ingredients list is a problem.
Doubtful
An ingredient could be fine or not, such as gelatine or E471, where the source isn't stated. Check the packaging for a halal mark.
Haram
Something that is not allowed, such as pork or alcohol, is in the list.

The app is a guide, not a religious ruling or a halal certificate. If you need a ruling, ask a qualified person. If you have an allergy, always read the label yourself.

Where the information comes from

Product names and ingredients come partly from Open Food Facts, a free, open database kept by volunteers, and partly from shoppers using the app.

When you photograph a label, an AI service reads the text. The photo is not kept. The ingredients are then checked against our own list of halal, doubtful and haram ingredients.
